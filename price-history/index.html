Amazon price history, explained

Updated November 9, 2026 · By Sam Barth

Price history is a record of what an item has actually sold for over the past weeks or months. It is the most reliable way to judge a sale, because it shows whether today's price is truly low or just lower than an inflated list price.

Why does price history matter more than the list price?

The list price is a reference number the seller chooses. Price history shows what buyers actually paid, so it tells you whether a sale is unusual or routine.

An item that sells at $40 most weeks and is marked "was $70, now $40" has not really dropped. An item that usually sells at $40 and drops to $29 has.

Can I see price history on Amazon itself?

Yes. Amazon says its Alexa for Shopping assistant shows 30-, 90- and 365-day price history. You can ask questions like "What's the price history?" or "Has this item been on sale in the past 30 days?"

Amazon also says Alexa for Shopping can set price alerts, and can buy an item automatically when it reaches a target price using your default payment method. If you use auto-buy, double-check the exact item and seller first. Source: Amazon News, updated October 29, 2026.

What about third-party price trackers?

Independent trackers such as Keepa and camelcamelcamel chart Amazon prices over time and send alerts when a price drops below a number you set.

They can show longer histories and separate prices from Amazon and from third-party sellers. Use them as a second opinion, and remember they show past prices, not a promise of future ones.

How do I read a price history chart?

Look for three things: the usual price (where the line sits most of the time), the recent low, and how often drops happen.

If today's price is near the recent low, it is a good price. If drops happen every few weeks, you can wait for the next one. If the line spiked right before a sale, the "discount" may just undo the spike.

Frequently asked questions

How far back should I check price history?

At least 90 days for everyday items. For seasonal items like coolers or holiday decor, look at a full year.

Do Amazon prices change during the day?

They can. Prices on popular items may change several times, which is why alerts are more useful than checking by hand.

Is the lowest price ever the only good price?

No. A price near the recent low on an item you need now is a good buy. Waiting for an all-time low can mean waiting for months.
