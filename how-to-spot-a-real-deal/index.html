How to spot a real deal on Amazon

Updated November 9, 2026 · By Sam Barth

A real deal is a price clearly below what the item has actually sold for recently. The crossed-out "list price" next to a sale tells you little on its own, so check price history, the seller and the exact model before you buy.

1. Is the price low compared with recent prices, not the list price?

Compare the sale price with the item's price over the last 90 days or more. If it sold at the "sale" price most of the year, it is not a real discount.

Amazon's own Alexa for Shopping assistant can show 30-, 90- and 365-day price history, according to Amazon, and third-party trackers chart prices too. Our price history guide shows how to read those charts.

2. Is the list price believable?

Be skeptical of a list price you have never seen anyone charge. A large percentage off an inflated reference price is a common way to make an ordinary price look special.

The Federal Trade Commission's Guides Against Deceptive Pricing (16 CFR Part 233) say a former-price comparison should be based on a price the item was actually offered at, in good faith, for a reasonably substantial period. That is a good test to apply yourself.

3. Is it the exact model you researched?

Check the model number, not just the product name. Sale listings sometimes feature an older, smaller or bundle-stripped version of a popular item.

Tool kits are a common example: a bare tool without batteries looks far cheaper than the full kit. Confirm what is in the box on the product page.

4. Who is the seller?

Look at the "Ships from" and "Sold by" lines near the buy button. Items sold by Amazon or by the brand's own store are the lowest-risk choice.

Unknown sellers offering a famous brand far below everyone else deserve extra caution, especially for chargers, batteries and anything that touches food or skin.

5. Is a countdown timer pushing you?

A timer creates urgency but does not change whether the price is good. If you already know the item's usual price, you can decide in seconds; if you do not, let it go.

Big sale events repeat. Prime Day, Prime Big Deal Days, Black Friday and Cyber Monday all happen every year, and many prices return between them.

6. Does a coupon or Subscribe and Save change the math?

Check the final price at checkout. A clipped coupon or Subscribe and Save discount can make an unadvertised item cheaper than a featured deal.

Read the terms on subscriptions so you are not signed up for repeat deliveries you do not want.

7. Would you buy it at this price if it were not on sale?

If the honest answer is no, skip it. The cheapest purchase is the one you did not need.

Make a list before a sale starts and stick to it. Our buying guides are a starting point for items worth owning.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good discount on Amazon?

There is no fixed percentage. A good discount is one that brings the price near or below the item's lowest recent price.

Are Lightning Deals real deals?

Some are, some are not. Treat them like any other sale: check the price history and seller first.

Can I get a refund if the price drops after I buy?

Amazon does not generally offer price matching on its own drops. Its holiday return window, through January 31, 2027 for most items bought November 1 to December 31, 2026, can give you room to return and rebuy.
